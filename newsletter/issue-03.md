## The jewel in the ring

Look at a map of the Persian Gulf. At its mouth, **between Iran and Oman**, the water pinches into a narrow gate: the **Strait of Hormuz**.

Almost every tanker leaving the Gulf has to pass through here. That's about **one barrel in every five** the world uses.

The strait is about **21 miles wide** at its narrowest. And the shipping lanes? **Just two miles wide each way.**

It mattered long before oil. In **1272**, **Marco Polo** passed through and saw ships from India unloading **spices, pearls and silk**. People said: *if all the world were a golden ring, Hormuz would be the jewel in it.*

And someone always wants the jewel. In **1507**, the Portuguese commander **Afonso de Albuquerque** arrived, burned the harbour's ships and forced the king to pay tribute. But three of his own captains sailed off, and he had to leave. He came back in **1515** with **twenty-seven ships**, and Hormuz gave in. For about a century, trade between India, Persia and Arabia had to deal with Portuguese Hormuz.

Persia's **Shah Abbas** wanted it back. He had a great army. But **he had no navy**.

So he **borrowed one**. In **1622** he made a deal with the **English East India Company**. English ships bombarded the fortress while Persian troops closed in. After about **ten weeks**, the Portuguese surrendered.

Then came oil. In **1908**, British prospectors struck oil in Persia, and soon tankers filled the Gulf. Today about **twenty million barrels a day** still squeeze through the same narrow gap.

So whoever threatens it can still shake the world.

## 3 things you didn't know

1. **Hormuz moved.** The city was first on the mainland. Around **1300**, to escape raiders, its rulers moved the whole city onto a **bare island** offshore.
2. **A port is named after the shah who won.** After 1622, trade moved to the mainland, to a port named after him: **Bandar Abbas**. Hormuz island slowly emptied.
3. **The 1980s "tanker war" was huge.** During the Iran–Iraq war, by the end of 1987 Iraq had attacked **283 ships** and Iran **168**.
