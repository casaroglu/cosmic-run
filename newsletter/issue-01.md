## The sandwich heist

**Antwerp, Belgium. February 2003.** In the city's diamond district, at the Diamond Centre, sat a vault that was about as safe as a room can be: **heat sensors, radar**, and a door lock with **a hundred million possible combinations**.

A gang got in anyway.

Over **one weekend**, they opened **more than a hundred safe deposit boxes**, one after another. Diamonds, gold, cash. All of it gone, about **$100 million** in total. It was the biggest diamond heist in history.

The plan was close to perfect. The getaway wasn't.

In a hurry, the thieves **dumped a trash bag in a forest by the highway**. A local man found it.

Inside were **receipts, a videotape**, and a **half-eaten salami sandwich**. And on that sandwich: a thief's **DNA**.

That was the thread that unravelled everything. **The thieves were caught.**

But here's the twist: **the diamonds were never found.** The treasure simply vanished.

The security could have stopped them. A sandwich did.

## 3 things you didn't know (from our Heists series)

1. **The biggest art heist ever is still unsolved.** In Boston in 1990, two men dressed as police officers took **13 works in 81 minutes**, including a Vermeer and Rembrandt's only seascape. The museum never replaced them, and **the empty frames still hang on the walls today**.
2. **Quebec keeps a strategic maple syrup reserve, like oil.** In 2012 an inspector climbed the barrels and nearly fell: they were empty. Thieves had drained about **3,000 tonnes**, worth **$18.7 million**, and refilled many barrels with water.
3. **England's biggest burglary was pulled off by pensioners.** In London in 2015, a gang mostly in their sixties and seventies drilled through **half a metre of concrete**. Police bugged their cars, and the gang couldn't stop bragging.
