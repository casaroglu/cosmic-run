## Columbus never knew

Did Christopher Columbus know he had found a new continent? **Never.** He died convinced he had reached **Asia**.

For years, nobody would back his plan to sail west. **Portugal turned him down.** Spain's experts said his numbers were wrong, and **they were right**: Columbus thought the Earth was much smaller than it is.

Then, in 1492, the city of Granada fell, and **Queen Isabella** finally said yes. Columbus drove a hard bargain. He demanded the title of **admiral**, a **governorship**, and **a tenth of the profits**. He got all three.

On **3 August 1492** he sailed west from **Palos** with **three ships and about ninety men**. Then came weeks of open ocean and nothing on the horizon. The crew came close to **mutiny**.

Then, on **12 October**, a lookout shouted: **land!** It was an island in the Bahamas, and Columbus named it **San Salvador**.

He called the people he met **"Indians"**, because he was sure he was in the Indies.

He sailed across the Atlantic **four times**. He died in **1506**, still insisting the lands he had reached were Asia.

A year later, a German mapmaker put the new land on a world map and named it after another explorer, **Amerigo Vespucci**: **America**.

So the land Columbus reached carries another explorer's name, not his.

## 3 things you didn't know

1. **Brazil speaks Portuguese because of a line in the ocean.** In **1494**, at **Tordesillas**, Spain and Portugal drew a line down the Atlantic: west for Spain, east for Portugal.
2. **1492 began with a surrender.** On **2 January 1492**, Granada's last sultan, **Boabdil**, handed over the keys of the city. Legend says he looked back from a mountain pass and wept, and that spot is still called **the Moor's Last Sigh**.
3. **Some exiles of 1492 still speak old Spanish.** That same year, Spain's Jews were ordered to convert or leave. Many found refuge in the **Ottoman Empire**, and their descendants, the Sephardim, still speak **Ladino**, a form of old Spanish.
