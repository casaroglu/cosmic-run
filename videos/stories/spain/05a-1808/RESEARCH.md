# Episode 5 — 1808, Part 1: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Weak Charles IV, hated favourite Godoy ran the country | Godoy chief minister / favourite from 1792 ✓ | Britannica "Manuel de Godoy"; Wikipedia "Tumult of Aranjuez" |
| Godoy let French troops cross Spain to attack Portugal | Treaty of Fontainebleau, 27 Oct 1807; Junot's corps crossed at Irún from 12 Oct, invaded Portugal Nov 1807 ✓ | Wikipedia "Treaty of Fontainebleau (1807)"; historyofwar.org "Junot's invasion of Portugal" |
| March 1808: mob storms Godoy's palace; two days later Charles abdicates for Ferdinand | Tumult of Aranjuez 17 Mar; abdication 19 Mar 1808 ✓ | Wikipedia "Tumult of Aranjuez"; Lumen "Abdication in Spain" |
| Napoleon invites father and son to Bayonne — a trap | Both went to Bayonne in April 1808 "under a pretext of settling the dispute" ✓ | Wikipedia "Abdications of Bayonne"; Lumen |
| 2 May 1808 Madrid rising when the last royals were being taken away | Spontaneous rising on 2 May (triggered by the removal of the remaining royal family members) ✓ | Museo del Prado (2nd of May entry); Wikipedia "Dos de Mayo Uprising" |
| That night the French shot prisoners | Murat's military commission (Grouchy) ordered those taken "arms in hand" shot; executions night of 2–3 May ✓ | Museo del Prado "The 3rd of May"; Wikipedia "The Third of May 1808" |
| Goya painted it six years later | Both canvases 1814 ✓ (Prado: after May 1814) | Museo del Prado; Fundación Goya en Aragón |
| Days later both kings signed away the crown; Joseph made king | Bayonne abdications 5–7 May 1808; Joseph king 6 June / proclaimed 15 June 1808 ✓ | Wikipedia "Abdications of Bayonne"; Britannica "Joseph Bonaparte" |

## Left out on purpose
- Number of people killed / executed on 2–3 May (estimates vary widely).
- Exact execution site (Príncipe Pío is traditional; the Prado doubts the painting shows it).
