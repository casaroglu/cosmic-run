# Episode 6 — The War of the Spanish Succession: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Charles II, last Habsburg king of Spain, died childless | d. 1 Nov 1700, no children ✓ | Lumen "The Question of Spanish Succession"; Hérodote "16 novembre 1700" |
| Will left everything to Philip of Anjou, grandson of Louis XIV; Louis accepted | Will of Oct 1700 named Philip, Duke of Anjou ✓; Louis XIV accepted and presented him at Versailles, 16 Nov 1700 ✓ | Lumen; Hérodote |
| England, the Dutch and Austria went to war | Grand Alliance (England, Dutch Republic, Austria) vs France/Spain, 1701–1714 ✓ | Lumen; National Army Museum "War of the Spanish Succession" |
| Rival king Archduke Charles; Spain split | Habsburg claimant "Charles III"; the Crown of Aragon largely backed him, Castile backed Philip ✓ | National Army Museum; Generalitat de Catalunya "Diada — efemèride" |
| Anglo-Dutch fleet took Gibraltar in the archduke's name, 4 Aug 1704 | Rooke & Hesse-Darmstadt; governor Salinas surrendered 4 Aug 1704; held "in the name of Charles III" ✓ | Britannica "Sir George Rooke"; MoneyWeek "4 August 1704" |
| Almansa decisive; Philip abolished the old laws of the kingdoms that fought him | Almansa 25 Apr 1707 (Berwick) ✓; Nueva Planta decrees: Aragon & Valencia 29 Jun 1707, Catalonia 1716 ✓ | Wikipedia "Battle of Almansa"; Museu d'Història de València "Under Bourbon Rule" |
| Utrecht 1713: Philip keeps Spain, loses Italy & Netherlands; Britain gets Gibraltar & Minorca | Treaty of Utrecht 1713, Art. X (Gibraltar), Art. XI (Minorca) ✓; Spanish Netherlands & Italian lands to Austria (Rastatt 1714) ✓ | Britannica "Peace of Utrecht"; National Army Museum |
| Barcelona fell 11 Sep 1714; Catalonia marks that day | Siege Jul 1713–11 Sep 1714 ✓; Diada Nacional de Catalunya ✓ | Generalitat de Catalunya; Spanish Army museum (Sala Sucesión) |
| First Bourbon king; Felipe VI is his descendant | Philip V (r. 1700–1746, except 1724) founded the Spanish Bourbon line; Felipe VI belongs to it ✓ | Britannica "Philip V"; Casa Real (casareal.es) |

## Left out on purpose
- "Il n'y a plus de Pyrénées" — attribution doubtful.
- Casualty / army sizes.
- Gibraltar's current political dispute (stated only as "still British").
