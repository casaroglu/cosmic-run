# Prequel — Las Navas de Tolosa: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1195 Almohads crushed Alfonso VIII at Alarcos | ✓ | Britannica "Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa"; Wikipedia |
| Seventeen years of preparation; the Pope proclaimed a crusade | 1195 → 1212 ✓; Innocent III's crusade bull ✓ | Wikipedia "Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa"; Military Wiki |
| Castile, Aragon and Navarre marched together | Alfonso VIII, Peter II, Sancho VII ✓ | Britannica; Wikipedia |
| Legend: a shepherd showed a hidden pass | Martín Alhaja story from Christian sources, later miraculous → labelled legend ✓ | Wikipedia; El Debate "La carga de los tres reyes" |
| 16 July 1212: charge on the Almohad camp; the caliph (al-Nasir) fled | ✓ | Britannica; Wikipedia |
| A silk banner in Burgos "said to have been taken that day" | Pendón de Las Navas, Monasterio de Las Huelgas; attribution traditional/disputed → script hedges ✓ | Wikipedia "Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa"; Patrimonio Nacional (Las Huelgas) |
| Within forty years Córdoba and Seville were Christian; Granada lasted until 1492 | Córdoba 1236, Seville 1248, Granada 1492 ✓ | Britannica "Reconquista" |

## Left out on purpose
- Sancho VII and the chains of Navarre's coat of arms (lore, weak sources).
- Army sizes and casualty figures (medieval exaggerations).
