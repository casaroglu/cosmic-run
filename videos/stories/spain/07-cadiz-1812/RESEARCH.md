# Episode 7 — The Constitution of Cádiz (1812): fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1810: French held most of Spain; Cádiz held out | Siege of Cádiz Feb 1810 – Aug 1812, never taken ✓ | Wikipedia "Spanish Constitution of 1812"; El Orden Mundial "19 de marzo de 1812" |
| King a prisoner in France; deputies from Spain and the Americas met (24 Sep 1810, Isla de León) | Ferdinand VII held at Valençay ✓; Cortes opened 24 Sep 1810 in the theatre of Isla de León (San Fernando), with American deputies ✓ | Congreso de los Diputados "Constitución de 1812"; Wikipedia |
| Sovereignty: the nation, not the king | "Sovereignty, previously held by the King, passes to the Nation" ✓ | Congreso de los Diputados |
| 19 Mar 1812, St Joseph's day, nicknamed La Pepa | ✓ | Congreso de los Diputados; El Orden Mundial |
| Power split in three; votes for men | Rigid separation of powers; indirect universal male suffrage ✓ | Congreso de los Diputados |
| Defenders called themselves *liberales*; the word spread | Political use of "liberal" began in the Cortes of Cádiz (1810–11) and spread to Britain and beyond ✓ (a Swedish precedent is claimed by a minority) | Hispanopedia "Liberalismo español"; Slugger O'Toole "A short history of the L word"; *Historia Constitucional* |
| Ferdinand tore it up (4 May 1814) | Decree of Valencia, 4 May 1814 ✓ | Univ. of Kent "1814 Fernando VII returns"; Congreso de los Diputados |
| 1820 Riego rebellion; king forced to swear | Riego at Las Cabezas de San Juan, 1 Jan 1820; Ferdinand swore the Constitution, Mar 1820 ✓ | Univ. of Warwick "Riego and the Cádiz pronunciamiento"; Congreso de los Diputados |
| 1823 French army restores the king; Riego executed in Madrid | Hundred Thousand Sons of Saint Louis, Apr 1823; Trocadero 31 Aug 1823; Riego executed 7 Nov 1823 ✓ | biografiasyvidas "Rafael de Riego"; Xunta "The reign of Fernando VII" |
| Ideas reached Portugal, Italy, Latin America | ✓ | Congreso de los Diputados |
