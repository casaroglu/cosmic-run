# The Ottoman Empire 11: the end of the Janissaries (1826) — fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1826: Mahmud II destroyed the Janissaries | ✓ 15 June 1826, corps disbanded | Wikipedia "Auspicious Incident"; Britannica "Ottoman Empire: The empire from 1807 to 1920" |
| Elite infantry founded in the 1300s; one of the first standing armies, salaried, armed with guns | ✓ (Orhan or Murad I; 1363 in the infobox) | Wikipedia "Janissary"; Wikipedia "Auspicious Incident" |
| Sons joined their fathers' units; many members ran shops | ✓ hereditary membership; "civilianized", trades | Wikipedia "Janissary"; executedtoday.com (1826 press report) |
| About 135,000 on the Janissary payroll by 1826 (c. 20,000 in 1575); many not real soldiers | ✓ | Wikipedia "Auspicious Incident"; Wikipedia "Janissary" |
| 1807: overthrew Selim III, who tried to build a modern army | ✓ (Nizam-ı Cedid) | Wikipedia "Janissary"; Britannica 1807–1920 |
| By 1826 Russia had Crimea; Greece in revolt | ✓ Crimea 1783; Greek revolt 1821 | Britannica 1807–1920; Wikipedia "Greek War of Independence" |
| June 1826: a new European-style army announced; the Janissaries revolted (Mahmud may have provoked it) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Auspicious Incident" (Kinross); Britannica ("mutinied in protest and were promptly massacred") |
| Barracks shelled and burned; thousands died | ✓ ~4,000 in the barracks; 6,000+ executed overall | Wikipedia "Auspicious Incident"; Military Wiki |
