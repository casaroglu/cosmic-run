## Fact check (two sources each)
| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| Nasal cells release a "swarm" of extracellular vesicles when they sense a virus; EVs carry virus-binding receptors (LDLR, ICAM-1) and act as decoys; carry antiviral miRNA (miR-17) | Huang, Bleier et al., *J Allergy Clin Immunol* 2023;151(2):509 (online Dec 2022, PubMed 36494212); Harvard Medical School news / Mass Eye and Ear press release (Dec 2022) |
| Volunteers moved from ~74°F room to 39.9°F (4.4°C) for 15 min; intranasal temperature fell ~5°C (~9°F); at that drop, EV secretion fell ~42% and EV antiviral binding was impaired; tested 2 rhinoviruses + 1 coronavirus; lab tissue, not infection study | Huang et al. JACI 2022/23; EurekAlert release 972940; HMS news |
| Rhinovirus causes most common colds; name = "nose virus" | CDC "Common Cold" page; Foxman et al. *PNAS* 2015 introduction |
| Nasal cavity ~33°C (~91°F) vs core 37°C; at 37°C infected mouse airway cells produced more type I/III interferon and restricted rhinovirus; mouse cells | Foxman, Iwasaki et al., *PNAS* 2015;112(3):827 (PubMed 25561542); Yale News, 5 Jan 2015 |
| Follow-up: most rhinovirus strains replicate better at 33–35°C than 37°C | Foxman et al. *PNAS* 2016; Yale News, 11 Jul 2016 |
| US flu activity begins rising in October, usually peaks Dec–Feb; February most common peak month (1982–2024) | CDC "Flu Season" page; Cleveland Clinic "When is flu season" |
| Guinea pigs at a New Mexico army base died in 1918 pandemic → Palese lab (Mount Sinai, NY) used them | Lowen et al. *PLoS Pathog* 2007;3(10):e151; CIDRAP news (2007) |
| Guinea pigs in adjoining cages: efficient transmission at 20–35% RH, none at 80%; 5°C (41°F) more transmission than 20°C, none at 30°C (86°F); peak shedding ~40 h longer at 5°C | Lowen et al. *PLoS Pathog* 2007 (PMC2034399); CIDRAP news (2007) |
| Dry air: droplets evaporate to small floating droplet nuclei, virus more stable; humid air keeps droplets heavy so they settle | Lowen et al. 2007; Moriyama, Hugentobler & Iwasaki, *Annu Rev Virol* 2020 |
| Absolute humidity explained ~90% of flu survival variance (vs 36% for RH) and ~50% of transmission | Shaman & Kohn, *PNAS* 2009;106(9):3243; Oregon State Univ. release / ScienceDaily (Feb 2009) |
| Winter outdoor and indoor absolute humidity are low; heated indoor air can fall below ~24–30% RH | Shaman & Kohn 2009; STAT News (Oct 2020, citing Taylor/Iwasaki measurements); Knowable Magazine (2023) |
| Mucociliary clearance: cilia move mucus up and out; mucus swallowed | Kudo et al. *PNAS* 2019; Moriyama et al. *Annu Rev Virol* 2020 |
| Mice at 10–20% RH vs 50% RH before flu: impaired mucociliary clearance, tissue repair and interferon-stimulated gene induction; more severe disease (study partly funded by humidifier maker Condair) | Kudo, Iwasaki et al. *PNAS* 2019 (PMC6561219); EurekAlert/Yale release (May 2019); Contagion Live |
| Americans spend ~90% of time indoors | EPA "Indoor Air Quality" (Report on the Environment); Klepeis et al. *J Expo Anal Environ Epidemiol* 2001 (NHAPS, 87%) |
| Crowding/indoor behavior amplifies but does not alone explain seasonality; poor ventilation lets airborne virus accumulate | Moriyama et al. *Annu Rev Virol* 2020; Univ. of Washington evidence summary; CDC "Ventilation in Buildings" |
| Ventilation and portable HEPA air cleaners reduce airborne virus particles | CDC "Ventilation in Buildings"; EPA "Air Cleaners and Air Filters in the Home" |
