# Chokepoints 6: the Bosphorus and the Dardanelles — fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The Turkish Straits are the Black Sea's only link to the oceans | ✓ | Wikipedia "Turkish Straits"; Britannica "Bosporus" |
| Bosphorus about 31 km long, about 700 m at its narrowest | ✓ 31 km; 700–750 m | Wikipedia "Bosporus"; WorldAtlas / Marine Insight |
| 1452: Mehmed II built Rumeli Hisarı; Constantinople fell in 1453 | ✓ built 1451–52 | Wikipedia "Rumelihisarı"; Britannica |
| Russia's Black Sea fleet wanted a way out; Britain and France wanted to keep it in | ✓ Straits Convention 1841, Crimean War | Wikipedia "Turkish Straits"; Britannica |
| 18 March 1915: mines sank three battleships, the Allied fleet turned back | ✓ Bouvet, Irresistible, Ocean | Wikipedia "Naval operations in the Dardanelles campaign"; Australian War Memorial |
| Montreux Convention, 1936: free passage for merchant ships in peacetime; Turkey controls warships | ✓ signed 20 July 1936 | Wikipedia "Montreux Convention"; Britannica |
| Non-Black Sea warships limited in tonnage, max 21 days in the Black Sea | ✓ | Wikipedia "Montreux Convention"; US Naval Institute |
| 2022: Turkey called the invasion of Ukraine a war, closed the straits to warships of the warring sides | ✓ announced 27–28 Feb 2022 (Art. 19) | Wikipedia; Reuters |
| Tens of thousands of ships a year pass Istanbul | ✓ ~40,000–48,000 per year (estimates vary) | Marine Insight; Wikipedia |
