# Chokepoints 7: the Strait of Malacca — fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| About a quarter of the world's traded goods pass through | ✓ ~25% | Wikipedia "Strait of Malacca"; EIA "World Oil Transit Chokepoints" |
| Between Malaysia and Sumatra, about 900 km long, down to Singapore | ✓ ~900 km (930 km) | Wikipedia; Britannica "Strait of Malacca" |
| Near Singapore the channel is under 3 km wide | ✓ 2.8 km at the Phillip Channel | Wikipedia; EIA |
| Around 1400 Parameswara founded Malacca; Chinese treasure fleets traded there | ✓ c. 1400; Zheng He's voyages 1405–1433 | Britannica "Malacca"; Wikipedia "Malacca Sultanate" |
| 1511 Portuguese took Malacca; 1641 the Dutch | ✓ Albuquerque, Aug 1511; Dutch siege ended Jan 1641 | Wikipedia "Capture of Malacca (1511)"; Britannica |
| 1819 Raffles set up a British port at Singapore | ✓ 6 Feb 1819 | Britannica "Stamford Raffles"; Wikipedia "Founding of modern Singapore" |
| 1824 Britain and the Netherlands split the strait | ✓ Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824 | Wikipedia; Britannica |
| Most of China's imported oil passes here; Hu Jintao's "Malacca dilemma" (2003) | ✓ roughly 80% of China's crude imports | Wikipedia; Lowy Institute / EIA |
| Detours via the Sunda or Lombok straits cost extra days | ✓ | EIA; Wikipedia "Lombok Strait" |
| Piracy in the early 2000s; joint patrols from 2004; attacks fell | ✓ Malacca Strait Patrols July 2004 | Wikipedia; IMB / ReCAAP reports |
