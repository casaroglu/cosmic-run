# 11 — Mansa Musa: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In 1324 he gave away so much gold its price fell | Arrived in Cairo July 1324 on hajj; al-Umari: mithqal fell from ≥25 dirhams to ≤22 ✓ | Wikipedia "Mansa Musa" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_I_of_Mali); Boston University African Studies Center "Kingdom of Mali" (al-Umari translation) (https://www.bu.edu/africa/outreach/teachingresources/history/k_o_mali/); Univ. of Houston Digital History "Mansa Musa's visit" (https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/active_learning/explorations/1492/mansa_musa_visit.cfm) |
| Mansa Musa ruled Mali, a West African empire rich in gold | Ruler of the Mali Empire c. 1312–1337; Mali a major gold supplier ✓ | Wikipedia; British Library "African kings on medieval and Renaissance maps" (https://www.bl.uk/stories/blogs/posts/african-kings-on-medieval-and-renaissance-maps) |
| He was going to Mecca | Hajj 1324–25 ✓ | Wikipedia; EBSCO Research Starters "Mansa Musa's Pilgrimage to Mecca" (https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/mansa-musas-pilgrimage-mecca) |
| Accounts describe thousands of followers and 100 camel loads of gold | DISPUTED numbers: 8,000 courtiers + 12,000 servants + 100 camel loads (Northwestern); 60,000 people, 100 baggage camels (EBSCO); Wikipedia calls such figures rhetorical → script says "accounts describe", "thousands" | Northwestern Magazine "A Golden Age King" (https://magazine.northwestern.edu/features/caravans-of-gold-fragments-in-time/a-golden-age-king-mansa-musas-reign); EBSCO; Wikipedia |
| In Cairo, every court official got a load of gold | Al-Umari: he left "no court emir nor holder of a royal office without the gift of a load of gold" ✓ | Boston University (al-Umari); Northwestern Magazine |
| Al-Umari, writing in Cairo, says gold stayed cheap about 12 years | "about twelve years" ✓. Historian W. Schultz argues the drop was within normal Mamluk fluctuation → script attributes it to al-Umari, says "prices fell" not "crashed" | Univ. of Houston Digital History; Boston University; Wikipedia |
| In 1375 a European map showed him holding gold | Catalan Atlas (conventionally 1375, Abraham Cresques): enthroned, holding a gold orb/coin/nugget ✓ (object's identity varies by source → "holding gold") | British Library; Wikipedia; EBSCO ("as early as 1375 … holding a gold nugget") |
| Historians still debate his wealth | "Richest person ever" is popular claim; historians (e.g. H. Collet) say it can't be calculated; BL: "sometimes said" ✓ | Wikipedia; British Library |
| Dropped | Borrowing money on the way home (only Wikipedia among the sources opened); title wording "crashed" softened to "price fell" in the script | — |
