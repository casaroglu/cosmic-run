# 08 — Operation Mincemeat: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In 1943 Britain needed Germany to ignore its real target, Sicily | Deception aimed to make Germany expect landings in Greece and Sardinia; Sicily was the real target ✓ | Wikipedia "Operation Mincemeat" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Mincemeat); National WWII Museum "Secret Agents, Secret Armies: Operation Mincemeat" (https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/secret-agents-secret-armies-operation-mincemeat) |
| The body was Glyndwr Michael, a homeless Welshman killed by rat poison | ✓ Homeless/tramp, died after eating rat poison (phosphorus). His age is given as 34 or 36 → script gives no age. One rival identity theory (HMS *Dasher* sailor) exists and is rejected by most historians; British government/CWGC recognise Michael | Wikipedia; National WWII Museum; History.co.uk "Operation Mincemeat" (https://www.history.co.uk/article/operation-mincemeat); Mental Floss "The Corpse That Fooled Hitler" (https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/world-war-ii/corpse-fooled-hitler) |
| Renamed Major William Martin (Royal Marines) | Captain (Acting Major) William Martin, Royal Marines ✓ | Wikipedia; History.co.uk |
| Pocket props: fiancée's photo, theatre tickets, ring receipt | Photo of "Pam", theatre ticket stubs, receipt for a diamond engagement ring ✓ (love letters also in Wikipedia but not in Mental Floss → script omits "love letters") | Wikipedia; Mental Floss |
| A submarine released him off Spain | HMS *Seraph* released the body off Huelva; found by a fisherman on 30 April 1943 (release date given as 29 or 30 April → script gives no date) ✓ | Wikipedia; National WWII Museum; History.co.uk |
| Carrying a letter pointing at Greece | Letter from Lt Gen Sir Archibald Nye to Gen Alexander named Greek landing sites, Sicily called a cover target ✓ | Wikipedia; National WWII Museum; History.co.uk |
| German agents copied the letter | Abwehr/German agents read and copied the documents, then they were returned ✓ | Wikipedia; History.co.uk; Mental Floss |
| Hitler sent tanks to Greece | Hitler ordered 1st Panzer Division from France to Greece (Salonika/Greece) ✓ | Wikipedia; National WWII Museum; History.co.uk |
| In July 1943 the Allies hit Sicily | Operation Husky, 9 July 1943 ✓ | Wikipedia; National WWII Museum |
| His Huelva grave now bears his real name | CWGC added "Glyndwr Michael served as Major William Martin RM" ✓ (year disputed: 1997 per Wikipedia/National WWII Museum, 1998 per History.co.uk → script says "now") | Wikipedia; National WWII Museum; History.co.uk |
