# 14 — Robert Fortune, the tea thief: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| In 1848 a Scottish botanist set out to steal China's tea | Left Southampton 20 June 1848 for China to obtain tea plants and makers ✓; Scottish, b. 1812 Berwickshire | Robert Fortune, *A Journey to the Tea Countries of China* (1852), preface (https://archive.org/details/journeytoteacoun00fort_0); Wikipedia "Robert Fortune" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fortune); NPR "Tea Tuesdays: The Scottish Spy Who Stole China's Tea Empire" (https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2015/03/10/392116370/tea-tuesdays-the-scottish-spy-who-stole-chinas-tea-empire) |
| Hired by the East India Company | ✓ | Fortune 1852 preface; Wikipedia; NPR; ExplorersWeb "How a Scottish Botanist Stole the Secrets of Tea from China" (https://explorersweb.com/how-a-scottish-botanist-stole-the-secrets-of-tea-from-china/) |
| China supplied the world's tea; Britain wanted its own, in India | "All the tea in the world came from China"; British planned a tea industry in India ✓ | NPR; ExplorersWeb |
| In disguise: shaved head, braid, Chinese clothes | Chinese dress, his "tail" (queue) dressed by a barber, head shaved ✓ | Fortune 1852, ch. II; ExplorersWeb; NPR (Chinese disguise) |
| He reached the tea hills | Green-tea district of Hwuy-chow (Anhui) and the Bohea/Wuyi hills ✓ | Fortune 1852; ExplorersWeb; Wikipedia (Fujian etc.) |
| Export green tea dyed with Prussian blue and gypsum | Fortune watched workers mix 4 parts gypsum to 3 parts Prussian blue into green tea for export ✓ | Fortune 1852, ch. V "Dyeing green teas"; ExplorersWeb |
| He shipped over 20,000 tea plants | "Upwards of twenty thousand tea-plants, eight first-rate manufacturers" (his own count) ✓; ExplorersWeb: nearly 20,000 bushes planted in India. Note: number comes from Fortune himself | Fortune 1852 preface; ExplorersWeb |
| His tea makers and know-how helped India's tea industry boom | Chinese tea makers taken to India ✓; transferred knowledge "instrumental" to later Indian tea industry; India later surpassed China as grower ✓ | Wikipedia; NPR; ExplorersWeb; Fortune 1852 preface |
| Dropped/softened | "Most of his plants died" — Wikipedia says most Chinese plants in NW India perished, but RBGE says Chinese forms were mainly grown in India → removed. Darjeeling/Assam destination differs between sources → script says only "India" | — |
