# 05 — Napoleon's height: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| He was about average | 1.67 m was "the period's average adult male height" (Wikipedia); "a little above average for a French man in the early 1800s" (History.com) ✓ | Wikipedia "Napoleon complex" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_complex); History.com "Was Napoleon Short? …" (https://www.history.com/articles/napoleon-complex-short) |
| His doctor and valet recorded him at five foot two | Valet Constant, Gen. Gourgaud and physician Antommarchi: "5 pieds 2 pouces" ✓ | History.com; Wikipedia ("5 feet 2 inches in pre-metric French measures") |
| French inches were longer | French pouce ≈ 1.06 English inches ✓ | National Geographic "Was Napoleon even short?…" (https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/napoleon-complex-height-discrimination-men-history); History.com (conversion to 1.67 m) |
| Britain was at war with him | Gillray's cartoons shrank "a threatening enemy" (wartime caricature) ✓ | National Geographic; History.com |
| About 1.67 m, roughly five foot six | 1.67 m / "just under 5′6″" (Wikipedia, History.com); "about 5′6″, autopsy confirmed" (Nat Geo); some historians say 5′7″/1.70 m → script says "roughly" | Wikipedia; History.com; National Geographic |
| Tall guardsmen (visual only) | Old Guard grenadiers had to be physically large; 1st Foot Grenadiers were the army's "tallest men" ✓ | National Geographic; Wikipedia "Imperial Guard (Napoleon I)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Guard_(Napoleon_I)) |
| From 1803, James Gillray drew him as "Little Boney" | 1803 prints ("Maniac Ravings – or – Little Boney in a Strong Fit"; "German Nonchalence, or the Vexation of Little Boney") ✓ | History.com; Aspects of History "Little Boney and the Satirist" (https://aspectsofhistory.com/little-boney-and-the-satirist/); Wikipedia (Gillray "popularized the myth") |
| A toddler throwing tantrums | Childlike / toddler-like figure in oversized clothes (Nat Geo); tantrum in "Maniac Ravings" (Aspects of History; History.com) ✓ | National Geographic; Aspects of History |
| Today we say "Napoleon complex" | Term for short men's supposed aggression, named after him ✓ | Wikipedia "Napoleon complex"; History.com / National Geographic (both articles are about the "Napoleon complex") |

**Dropped:** Napoleon's line about Gillray — History.com: "did more than all the armies of Europe to bring me down"; Aspects of History: "more damage than a dozen British generals". Wording differs, so not quoted. "Little Corporal" nickname (Nat Geo only) not used. Exact height in English inches (5′6″ vs 5′7″) softened to "roughly five foot six".
