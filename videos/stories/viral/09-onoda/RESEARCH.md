# 09 — Hiroo Onoda: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Onoda fought on until 1974 | Relieved of duty 9 March 1974, surrendered 10–11 March 1974 ✓ | Wikipedia "Hiroo Onoda" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroo_Onoda); CBS News "WWII soldier who hid in jungle for 29 years dies at 91" (https://www.cbsnews.com/news/wwii-soldier-who-hid-in-jungle-for-29-years-dies-at-91) |
| In 1944 Japan sent him to Lubang Island, Philippines | Sent 26 Dec 1944; Lubang c. 150 km SW of Manila ✓ | Wikipedia; CBS News |
| His orders: never surrender; he hid in the jungle | Orders barred surrender (and suicide); after Allied landing Feb 1945 he withdrew to the mountains/jungle ✓ | Wikipedia; CBS News; The Week "Hiroo Onoda, 1922–2014" (https://theweek.com/articles/452347/hiroo-onoda-19222014) |
| Leaflets told him; he called them enemy propaganda | ✓ Leaflets dismissed as propaganda/tricks. (Historian B. Trefalt doubts he truly didn't know — not in script) | Wikipedia; The Week; CBS News (leaflets ignored) |
| He raided villages, killing local people | ✓ Raided villages, killed locals he took for enemies; "up to 30" in local accounts → number disputed, script gives none | Wikipedia; The Week |
| In 1974 traveller Norio Suzuki found him | Met 20 Feb 1974 ✓ | Wikipedia; CBS News |
| Onoda demanded orders; Major Taniguchi flew in to relieve him | Maj. Yoshimi Taniguchi flew to Lubang and formally relieved him ✓ | Wikipedia; CBS News; The Week |
| He surrendered his sword | Surrendered in uniform with sword to President Marcos, 11 March 1974 ✓ (Marcos pardon only in Wikipedia → dropped) | Wikipedia; CBS News |
| Almost 29 years late | ~28 years 6 months after Japan's surrender (Wikipedia); "29 years" (CBS headline) → "almost 29" ✓ | Wikipedia; CBS News |
