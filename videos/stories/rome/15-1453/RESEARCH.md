# Ancient Rome 15 (series finale): 1453, the last Roman emperor: fact check

| Claim | Fact | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Istanbul's Theodosian land walls, about 5.7 km from the Golden Horn to the Sea of Marmara, still stand ("almost six kilometres"); built in the early 400s (first phase finished 413) | ✓ UNESCO World Heritage (1985), partly ruined/restored | Wikipedia "Walls of Constantinople"; World History Encyclopedia "Theodosian Walls" (both in `ottoman/04a-walls/RESEARCH.md`) |
| "Byzantine Empire" is a later historians' name; its people called themselves Romans (*Rhomaioi*) and the state the Roman Empire | ✓ (the label was popularised in the 16th century, usually credited to Hieronymus Wolf, 1557) | Wikipedia "Byzantine Empire" ("coined only after its demise; its citizens used the term 'Roman Empire'"); Wikipedia "Hieronymus Wolf"; Cambridge, *Byzantine and Modern Greek Studies* 2020 "Did the Byzantines call themselves Byzantines?" |
| The capital was the city Constantine founded (dedicated 11 May 330) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Constantinople"; ep. 13 `RESEARCH.md` |
| The last emperor was Constantine XI Palaiologos, proclaimed 6 January 1449 (at Mystras) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Constantine XI Palaiologos"; Wikipedia "Fall of Constantinople" |
| His mother was Helena (Helena Dragaš); Constantine I's mother was also Helena | ✓ | Wikipedia "Constantine XI Palaiologos"; Wikipedia "Helena Dragaš"; World History Encyclopedia "Helena of Constantinople"; Wikipedia "Helena, mother of Constantine I" |
| By 1451 the empire was the city plus the Morea (southern Greece) and a few islands; the Ottoman Empire all around | ✓ | Wikipedia "Fall of Constantinople"; Wikipedia "Despotate of the Morea" (`ottoman/04a-walls`) |
| Siege began 6 April 1453; Mehmed II was 21 (born 30 March 1432); giant cannons (Orban) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Fall of Constantinople"; Wikipedia "Mehmed II"; HistoryNet "The Guns of Constantinople" |
| Up to 80,000 Ottoman soldiers; about 7,000 defenders | ✓ modern estimates 50–80,000; Sphrantzes 4,773 Greeks + ~200 foreigners + volunteers | Wikipedia "Fall of Constantinople"; Britannica "Siege of Constantinople" (`ottoman/04a-walls`) |
| The siege lasted 53 days (6 April – 29 May) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Fall of Constantinople" ("53-day siege"); World History Encyclopedia "1453: The Fall of Constantinople" |
| Ships dragged overland behind Galata into the Golden Horn (22 April), around the chain | ✓ about 70 ships | Wikipedia "Fall of Constantinople"; Stanton, *Medieval Maritime Warfare* (`ottoman/04b-ships`) |
| Final attack before dawn, 29 May 1453 | ✓ | Wikipedia "Fall of Constantinople"; World History Encyclopedia |
| Constantine XI died in the fighting; his body was never identified | ✓ no reliable eyewitness; most accounts: a last stand. Body never found for certain | Wikipedia "Constantine XI Palaiologos"; GreekReporter "The Legend of the Undead Last Byzantine Emperor"; UC Libraries blog "Fall of Constantinople" |
| Greek folklore: an angel turned him to marble and he sleeps under (near) the Golden Gate, waiting to return ("the Marble Emperor") | ✓ told as a legend | Wikipedia "Constantine XI Palaiologos" ("Marble Emperor"); GreekReporter "The Legend of the Undead Last Byzantine Emperor Destined to Return" |
| The photochrom wall at Yedikule ("Irdikale") is the stretch where the Golden Gate stands | ✓ | Wikipedia "Golden Gate (Constantinople)"; Wikipedia "Yedikule Fortress" |
| Mehmed II took the title *Kayser-i Rûm*, "Caesar of Rome" | ✓ (used sparingly in documents; last official use in the 18th c.; the script does not claim how long) | Wikipedia "Ottoman claim to Roman succession"; Britannica "Mehmed II: Mehmed's empire" |
| From 753 BC (traditional founding of Rome, the wolf legend) to AD 1453 ≈ 22 centuries (2,205 years) | ✓ arithmetic | Wikipedia "Founding of Rome"; ep. 01 |

Left out on purpose: how exactly Constantine XI died (accounts differ), the Kerkoporta story, the three days of plunder, the Empire of Trebizond (1461) and the Morea (1460), Hagia Sophia's conversion.
"The Roman Empire ended in Istanbul in 1453" is the mainstream convention (Wikipedia "Byzantine Empire": "The fall of Constantinople to the Ottomans in 1453 brought the empire to an end").

## Borders (sketch maps, approximate)
- **Start, 1451:** the Roman Empire at its height (AD 117, faint red; polygon from ep. 10) against the Ottoman Empire c. 1451 (green, from `ottoman/territory.json` "1451") and what was left of Rome (blue): Constantinople and the Morea. Markers Constantinople, Mystras.
- **End, 1453:** AD 117 faint, the Ottoman Empire including Constantinople, arrow Rome → Istanbul, "Rome, 753 BC" and "Istanbul, 1453". The Morea (until 1460) is left out of the end map on purpose to keep it readable.
- Coastline: Natural Earth.
