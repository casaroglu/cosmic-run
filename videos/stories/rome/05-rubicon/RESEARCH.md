# Ancient Rome 5: Crossing the Rubicon (49 BC): fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Caesar crossed in January 49 BC (traditionally the night of 10–11 January) | ✓ (exact day uncertain) | Wikipedia "Crossing the Rubicon"; Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 31–32; Plutarch, *Caesar* 32 |
| Small river between Rimini and Cesena, ~80 km long | ✓ | Wikipedia "Rubicon"; livius.org "Rubico (49 BCE)" |
| Gaul conquered in ~8 years (58–50 BC), incl. today's France and Belgium; Vercingetorix surrendered at Alesia, 52 BC | ✓ | Wikipedia "Gallic Wars"; Wikipedia "Battle of Alesia" |
| Rubicon = border of Italy (Cisalpine Gaul to the north); a general entering Italy with troops lost imperium; punishable by death | ✓ | Wikipedia "Crossing the Rubicon"; Wikipedia "Rubicon" |
| Senate (Pompey's faction) ordered Caesar to give up his command; as a private citizen he faced prosecution | ✓ | Wikipedia "Crossing the Rubicon"; Wikipedia "Caesar's civil war" |
| One legion, the 13th | ✓ | Wikipedia "Crossing the Rubicon"; Wikipedia "Legio XIII Gemina" |
