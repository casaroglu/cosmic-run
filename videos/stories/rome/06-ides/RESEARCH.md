# Ancient Rome 6: The Ides of March (44 BC): fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Killed 15 March 44 BC (Ides) by senators | ✓ | Wikipedia "Assassination of Julius Caesar"; Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 80–82 |
| In the Curia of Pompey (Theatre of Pompey); site in Largo di Torre Argentina, now a cat sanctuary (founded 1993, ~350 cats) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Largo di Torre Argentina"; Wikipedia "Assassination of Julius Caesar" |
| Named dictator perpetuo in early 44 BC; fear he would become king | ✓ | Wikipedia "Assassination of Julius Caesar"; Plutarch, *Caesar* 57, 60–61 |
| 60+ conspirators led by Brutus and Cassius | ✓ | Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 80; Wikipedia "Assassination of Julius Caesar" |
| Caesar pardoned Brutus after Pharsalus and favoured him | ✓ | Plutarch, *Brutus* 6–8; Wikipedia "Marcus Junius Brutus" |
| Spurinna's warning; "the Ides have come" / "but not gone"; Calpurnia's dream | ✓ | Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 81; Plutarch, *Caesar* 63 |
| Daggers under togas; fell at the foot of Pompey's statue | ✓ | Plutarch, *Caesar* 66; Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 82 |
| 23 wounds; physician Antistius: only the second was fatal (first recorded autopsy) | ✓ | Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 82; Wikipedia "Assassination of Julius Caesar" |
| "Et tu, Brute?" is Shakespeare (1599); Suetonius: silence, or Greek "kai su, teknon" | ✓ | Suetonius, *Divus Julius* 82; Wikipedia "Et tu, Brute?" |
| Brutus's EID MAR denarius (two daggers, cap of liberty), 42 BC | ✓ | Wikipedia "Eid Mar"; British Museum coin record |
| Philippi 42 BC: Octavian and Antony defeat Brutus and Cassius, who take their own lives | ✓ | Wikipedia "Battle of Philippi"; Plutarch, *Brutus* 43, 52 |
| July (Quintilis) renamed for Caesar; Kaiser and Tsar derive from "Caesar" | ✓ | Wikipedia "July"; Wikipedia "Caesar (title)"; Etymonline "Kaiser", "czar" |
