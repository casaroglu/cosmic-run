# Ancient Rome 11: Hadrian's Wall (AD 122): fact check

| Claim | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 73 miles (80 Roman miles, 117 km), Wallsend/Newcastle on the Tyne to Bowness-on-Solway | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; English Heritage FAQ / "History of Hadrian's Wall" |
| Lies wholly in England; never the Anglo-Scottish border | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; English Heritage FAQ |
| Begun AD 122 under Hadrian, the year he visited Britain | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; English Heritage "History" |
| Hadrian was Trajan's successor; gave up Trajan's eastern conquests and preferred defensible frontiers | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian"; Britannica "Hadrian" |
| Biography says the wall was "to separate the Romans from the barbarians" | ✓ | *Historia Augusta*, Hadrian 11.2 (4th-century, hence "an old biography"); Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall" |
| A milecastle (small fort with a gate) every Roman mile; two turrets between each; gates could monitor and tax movement ("probably") | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; English Heritage "History" |
| Built largely in about six years by three legions (II Augusta, VI Victrix, XX Valeria Victrix) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; English Heritage ("at least six years") |
| 80 milecastles, 16 forts | ✓ | English Heritage FAQ (16 forts, 80 milecastles); Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall" (lists 16 forts) |
| Auxiliaries from across the empire, e.g. Batavians and Tungrians (today's Netherlands and Belgium) | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall" (cohors I Batavorum); Wikipedia "Vindolanda" / "Housesteads Roman Fort" (cohors I Tungrorum) |
| Vindolanda tablets: wooden letters, older than the wall | ✓ | Wikipedia "Vindolanda tablets"; British Museum |
| Birthday invitation from Claudia Severa to Sulpicia Lepidina, c. AD 97–103; perhaps the oldest known Latin written by a woman | ✓ (hedged "perhaps") | Wikipedia "Vindolanda tablets"; Live Science (2024); British Museum 1986,1001.64 |
| Antonine Wall begun c. 142 under Antoninus Pius; abandoned c. 162, Hadrian's Wall reoccupied (by 164) | ✓ ("twenty years later") | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; Wikipedia "Antonine Wall" |
| Held until the end of Roman rule in Britain, c. 410 | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; English Heritage "History" |
| UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987 | ✓ | Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall"; English Heritage FAQ |
| The whole line can be walked in about a week (Hadrian's Wall Path, 84 miles, usually 6–7 days) | ✓ | National Trails "Hadrian's Wall Path"; Wikipedia "Hadrian's Wall Path" |

## Borders
- Opening map: the wall line (Wallsend–Bowness) and today's England–Scotland border (Solway–Carter Bar–Tweed), to show they are different.
- AD 122 map: Roman Britain south of the wall (outpost forts north of it left out), Caledonia unconquered.
- End map: Roman Britain to Hadrian's Wall (solid), the Antonine advance to the Forth–Clyde line in 142 (faint), abandoned c. 162, Hadrian's Wall held until c. 410. Borders are approximate.

## Left out on purpose
- Regina's tombstone at South Shields (Barates of Palmyra): no PD/CC0 photo found.
- Turret total (156 vs 158 in different sources): only "two turrets between each" is said.
