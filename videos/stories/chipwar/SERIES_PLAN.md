# Chip Wars: series plan (draft, 2026-11-25)

15 Shorts (vertical 1080x1920, ~45-55 s each), joined into 3 long-form parts (16:9, ~9-10 min each), the same way the Rome series was built.
Because every frame is drawn in code, the long parts are **re-rendered at 16:9** with the same beats and a wider camera, instead of pillarboxing the vertical Shorts.

The chain: each Short ends on the thing the next one picks up. The Shorts never tease the next episode (History Sketches rule), but cut together they read as one story.

- Part 1 **How a Chip Is Made**: from sand to silicon. Five chokepoints, five places on the map.
- Part 2 **The Chip War**: Washington cuts China off, China cuts back, and a start-up shakes the market.
- Part 3 **The AI Gold Rush**: Nvidia, power, cables and the labs racing to build the smartest machine.

Style: the collage map from the style test (engraved map, halftone cut-outs, Blender 3D props, raised yellow region, ransom letters, marker notes).
Voice: Kokoro Q&A (Q asks, A answers), faster and livelier than ep 01. Which voice is the user's pick from the three samples.
Every Short: payoff in the first 1.5 s, last line loops into the first, hidden 1 s key-fact card, CTA + corner logo, Gemini cover with a 4-5 word hook.

Facts: all lines below use facts with two independent sources (research packs, 2026-11-25). Disputed figures are worded as the safe version.
Items marked **[recheck]** can change before upload.

## PART 1 · HOW A CHIP IS MADE

### 01 · The Town That Makes Every Chip Possible (Spruce Pine)
Map: Appalachians, pin Spruce Pine NC (35.92, -82.07) → wide world arrows to fabs.
Hook card: **ONE TOWN. EVERY CHIP.**
- A: Almost every computer chip on Earth starts in a bowl made of quartz!
- Q: A bowl? Made where?
- A: Most of that quartz comes from one tiny town: Spruce Pine, North Carolina.
- Q: Why there?
- A: Its rock is so pure that analysts say it supplies around 80 percent of the world's high-purity quartz.
- A: Silicon is melted in quartz crucibles at over 1,400 degrees. Any impurity would ruin the crystal.
- Q: And only two companies mine it?
- A: Two! Sibelco and The Quartz Corp.
- A: Then, in September 2024, Hurricane Helene cut the roads, the rail and the power.
- Q: And the chip world panicked?
- A: The mines were shut. Sibelco restarted two weeks later.
- A: So next time you hold a phone, remember: it started in a quartz bowl.  *(loops to line 1)*
Hidden card: SPRUCE PINE ≈ 80% OF HIGH-PURITY QUARTZ (BNEF ESTIMATE)

### 02 · The Gas From a War Zone (Ukraine's neon)
Map: Black Sea, pins Odesa (46.48, 30.73) and Mariupol (47.10, 37.55) → arrows to Asia/US fabs.
Hook card: **HALF THE NEON. ONE WAR.**
- A: Half the world's chip-grade neon came from just two companies in Ukraine!
- Q: Neon? Like the signs?
- A: Same gas. Chipmaking lasers are filled with it, to draw circuits with light.
- A: The two firms sat in Odesa and Mariupol.
- Q: Mariupol... the city that was besieged in 2022?
- A: Exactly. Both plants stopped when the invasion began.
- Q: So the chip factories stopped too?
- A: No! After Crimea in 2014, prices had jumped 600 percent, so chipmakers had already found other suppliers.
- A: Neon comes from steel mills, where it's filtered out of the air. China and Korea built their own.
- A: And today some lasers recycle their neon, again and again.
- A: The lesson? A war two thousand kilometres away can reach every chip.  *(loops: "half the world's neon")*
Hidden card: UKRAINE ≈ 45-54% OF CHIP-GRADE NEON BEFORE 2022

### 03 · The Machine Only One Company Can Build (ASML) — remake of ep 01, richer
Map: Netherlands raised, 3D machine turning; arrows from Oberkochen and San Diego; arc to Hsinchu.
Hook card: **50,000 SHOTS A SECOND**
- A: This machine fires a laser at a falling drop of tin, fifty thousand times a second!
- Q: Why would anyone do that?
- A: Each hit makes a flash of light hotter than the surface of the Sun. That light prints the circuits on the world's best chips.
- Q: Who builds it?
- A: Only one company on Earth: A, S, M, L, in Veldhoven, the Netherlands.
- A: Its mirrors come from Zeiss in Germany. Blown up to the size of Germany, the biggest bump would be a tenth of a millimetre!
- Q: And how do you deliver it?
- A: Forty containers, twenty trucks, three cargo jets. The newest version costs around 400 million dollars.
- A: More than half of them work for one chipmaker in Taiwan.
- Q: And China?
- A: Never received one. In 2019 the Dutch let China's licence lapse.
- A: All of it, from one drop of tin, fifty thousand times a second.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: TSMC RUNS ~56% OF ALL EUV MACHINES (2024)

### 04 · The Company That Never Made a Chip (Arm, Cambridge)
Map: England, pin Cambridge (52.20, 0.18) → dots spreading over every continent (phones).
Hook card: **NEVER MADE ONE CHIP**
- A: This company has never made a single chip, yet it designed the brain of 99 percent of smartphones!
- Q: How is that possible?
- A: Arm, from Cambridge, England, sells designs, not chips.
- A: Apple, Qualcomm, Samsung pay a licence, then a small fee on every chip they make.
- Q: How many chips is that?
- A: Over 350 billion so far.
- Q: Where did it start?
- A: In 1990, as a joint venture of Acorn, Apple and VLSI, with twelve engineers.
- A: In 2016 Japan's SoftBank bought it for 24 billion pounds.
- A: Then Nvidia tried to buy it for 40 billion dollars. Regulators in America, Britain and Europe pushed back, and the deal died in 2022.
- A: Not bad, for a company that has never made a single chip.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: ARM CPUs IN 99%+ OF SMARTPHONES (ARM ANNUAL REPORT)

### 05 · The Island That Makes the Future (Taiwan / TSMC)
Map: Taiwan raised, the Strait dimension line, arrows from Arizona, Nvidia, Apple.
Hook card: **90% · ONE ISLAND**
- A: Around 90 percent of the world's most advanced chips come from one company, on one island!
- Q: Which island?
- A: Taiwan. The company is TSMC, in Hsinchu.
- Q: How close is it to China?
- A: The strait is only about 130 to 160 kilometres wide. Less than a hundred miles.
- A: Morris Chang founded TSMC in 1987 with a strange idea: make chips only for other people.
- A: Today it takes about 70 percent of all money paid to chip factories.
- Q: Who's the biggest customer?
- A: Since 2025, Nvidia, ahead of Apple.
- A: So America asked TSMC to build at home. It pledged 165 billion dollars for Arizona.
- Q: Does Arizona make the best chips yet?
- A: Not yet. The very best are still made on one island.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: TSMC ≈ 72.5% OF FOUNDRY REVENUE (Q2 2026)

## PART 2 · THE CHIP WAR

### 06 · The Day Washington Pulled the Plug (7 Oct 2022)
Map: Washington DC → Beijing; Nvidia Santa Clara; chips ping-pong.
Hook card: **7 OCT 2022**
- A: On the 7th of October 2022, Washington cut China off from the best AI chips.
- Q: Just like that?
- A: New rules blocked Nvidia's top chips, and the tools to make them.
- Q: So what did Nvidia do?
- A: It made slower versions just for China, the A800 and H800.
- A: A year later the rules tightened, and those were banned too.
- A: Then came the H20. In April 2025 that needed a licence too. Nvidia wrote off 4.5 billion dollars.
- Q: And now?
- A: Now Washington lets some chips through, and keeps 25 percent of the sales.
- Q: From ban to tax?
- A: Exactly. And it all started on the 7th of October 2022.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: US TAKES 25% OF H200 SALES TO CHINA (DEC 2025)

### 07 · The Chip Smugglers (Operation Gatekeeper, Supermicro case)
Map: US West Coast → Malaysia/Thailand/Singapore → China, dashed smuggling trails, fake labels.
Hook card: **FAKE BRAND. REAL CHIPS.**
- A: Smugglers relabelled top Nvidia chips with a fake brand name and shipped them as computer parts!
- Q: How much?
- A: At least 160 million dollars' worth, between 2024 and 2025. The organiser pleaded guilty.
- Q: Where did they go?
- A: Through South-East Asia, Malaysia and Thailand, toward China.
- A: In another case, supercomputers packed with H100s were caught on the way.
- Q: Anything bigger?
- A: In March 2026, a co-founder of Supermicro was arrested. Prosecutors allege 2.5 billion dollars of servers, with dummy machines to fool inspectors.
- A: Those are allegations, not convictions.
- A: But the lesson is clear: when a chip is banned, it gets a fake brand name.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: "OPERATION GATEKEEPER": $160M+ OF NVIDIA CHIPS (GUILTY PLEA, OCT 2025)

### 08 · China Strikes Back: Gallium (gallium & germanium)
Map: China raised, Beijing MOFCOM, Yunnan; arrows to Rotterdam with price tags.
Hook card: **99% FROM ONE COUNTRY**
- A: China makes 99 percent of the world's gallium. And in 2023, it started using that.
- Q: What is gallium even for?
- A: Chips, LED lights, and the radars on modern air-defence systems.
- A: Its cousin germanium carries light through fibre-optic cables, and sharpens night-vision lenses.
- Q: So what did China do?
- A: In August 2023, it required licences. In December 2024, it banned exports to America outright.
- Q: And the prices?
- A: Gallium more than doubled in Europe. Germanium hit about 5,000 dollars a kilo, five times its 2023 price.
- A: After a Trump-Xi truce, the US ban was paused. **[recheck: pause ends 27 Nov 2026]**
- A: But the lesson stays: 99 percent from one country is a weapon.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: CHINA = 99% OF PRIMARY GALLIUM (USGS 2026)

### 09 · The Magnets That Stopped Car Factories (rare earths)
Map: Bayan Obo, Inner Mongolia → Chicago Ford plant X; Europe lines X; Mountain Pass CA; Mt Weld / Malaysia.
Hook card: **-74% IN ONE MONTH**
- A: In May 2025, China's magnet exports fell 74 percent in a single month!
- Q: Magnets? Why does that matter?
- A: Rare-earth magnets are in every electric motor, car, drone and data-centre fan.
- A: China refines about 90 percent of these metals, and makes 94 percent of the magnets.
- Q: What happened?
- A: China put licences on seven rare earths. Within weeks Ford idled its Chicago plant, and European suppliers shut lines.
- Q: Did the West fight back?
- A: The Pentagon bought into a California mine, and guaranteed it a price.
- A: In Malaysia, Lynas became the first to make heavy rare earths outside China.
- A: But China still makes 94 percent of the magnets. **[recheck: pause runs to 10 Jan 2027]**
- A: That's why one bad month can stop car factories.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: CHINA MAKES 94% OF RARE-EARTH MAGNETS (IEA)

### 10 · The Start-up That Wiped 590 Billion (DeepSeek)
Map: Hangzhou pin → arc to New York, a falling ransom number.
Hook card: **-$590,000,000,000**
- A: One Chinese app wiped about 590 billion dollars off Nvidia in a single day!
- Q: Which app?
- A: DeepSeek, from Hangzhou. In January 2025 its free model rivalled the best in America.
- A: It hit number one on the US App Store, ahead of ChatGPT.
- Q: How did it do that without the best chips?
- A: It trained on H800s, the slower chips made for China, before they were banned too.
- A: It said its final training run cost about 5.6 million dollars in rented chip time. Analysts say the real total was far higher.
- Q: So the bans failed?
- A: Experts say they leaked, not failed.
- A: And in 2026 its new model was built to run on Chinese Huawei chips.
- A: All of it from one free app, and one very bad day for Nvidia.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: NVIDIA -17% ON 27 JAN 2025, BIGGEST ONE-DAY LOSS IN US HISTORY

## PART 3 · THE AI GOLD RUSH

### 11 · From a Denny's Booth to 5 Trillion (Nvidia's origin)
Map: San Jose Denny's pin → Toronto (AlexNet) → wide globe, ransom "$5,000,000,000,000".
Hook card: **BORN IN A DENNY'S**
- A: The most valuable company on Earth was planned in a Denny's booth!
- Q: The diner?
- A: In 1993, three engineers met there in San Jose to design a 3D graphics chip.
- A: They called it Nvidia. It made chips for video games.
- Q: So how did games turn into AI?
- A: In 2006 Nvidia unveiled CUDA, so its chips could do any maths, not just graphics.
- A: In 2012, a Toronto team trained an AI called AlexNet on two gaming cards, and it crushed the competition.
- Q: And then?
- A: Nvidia touched one trillion dollars in 2023, four trillion in 2025, and became the first ever five-trillion-dollar company that October.
- A: Denny's even put a plaque above the booth.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: NVIDIA: FIRST $5 TRILLION COMPANY (29 OCT 2025)
Note: avoid stating Huang's current role (resigned 12 Nov 2026, reinstated 23 Nov). The founding story names no role.

### 12 · Why Nobody Can Catch Nvidia
Map: Santa Clara raised; TSMC packaging plant Zhunan; rivals as pins (Google, Amazon, AMD) with queues.
Hook card: **$107 BILLION · 3 MONTHS**
- A: Nvidia made 107 billion dollars from data centres in just three months!
- Q: Why can't anyone compete?
- A: Roughly eight in ten AI chips are Nvidia's. But the real secret is software.
- A: CUDA. Twenty years old, eleven million developers. Switching chips means rewriting everything.
- Q: But Google and Amazon make their own chips.
- A: They do! Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium. AI labs rent both by the hundreds of thousands.
- A: But they all wait in the same queue, at TSMC's packaging lines in Taiwan.
- Q: So even rivals depend on the same island?
- A: Yes. And Nvidia books about 60 percent of that line. **[analyst estimate]**
- A: That's how you make 107 billion dollars in three months.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: CUDA: 11M+ DEVELOPERS (NVIDIA, NOV 2026)

### 13 · AI Is Eating the Power Grid
Map: Ireland raised (23%); Three Mile Island pin; Abilene TX; Memphis turbines.
Hook card: **23% OF A COUNTRY'S POWER**
- A: In Ireland, data centres now use 23 percent of all the country's electricity!
- Q: Almost a quarter?
- A: Nearly as much as every home in Ireland combined. In 2015 it was five percent.
- A: Worldwide, data centres may use about twice as much power by 2030.
- Q: So where does the power come from?
- A: Microsoft is restarting a reactor at Three Mile Island, and buying all of it for 20 years.
- A: Meta signed nuclear deals for up to 6.6 gigawatts.
- A: In Texas, one AI campus was planned for over a gigawatt, about a big nuclear reactor's worth.
- Q: And the water?
- A: Hundreds of billions of litres a year, to keep the chips cool.
- A: All so that Ireland's servers can use 23 percent of its power.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: IRELAND DATA CENTRES: 23% OF METERED ELECTRICITY (CSO, 2025)

### 14 · The Internet Lives Under the Sea
Map: world ocean with cable lines; Red Sea zoom: drifting ship, anchor trail, X on 3 cables; Baltic X.
Hook card: **ONE ANCHOR. 25%.**
- A: One sinking ship's anchor cut a quarter of the Red Sea's internet traffic!
- Q: The internet isn't in space?
- A: Satellites carry under one percent. Over 99 percent of data between continents runs through cables on the sea floor.
- A: About 600 of them.
- Q: What happened in the Red Sea?
- A: In 2024 a cargo ship, hit by a Houthi missile, drifted, dragging its anchor, and cut three cables.
- A: In 2025, more cables were cut near Jeddah.
- A: In the Baltic, at least ten cables have been damaged since 2023.
- Q: Who owns these cables?
- A: More and more, Google, Meta, Microsoft and Amazon. They use about three quarters of the bandwidth.
- A: And one anchor can still cut a quarter of it.  *(loops)*
Hidden card: 99%+ OF INTERCONTINENTAL DATA GOES BY CABLE (TELEGEOGRAPHY)

### 15 · The Race to Build the Smartest Machine (the labs + the record IPO)
Map: world pins: San Francisco (OpenAI, Anthropic), London (DeepMind), Menlo Park (Meta), Hangzhou (DeepSeek), Paris (Mistral); zoom New York for the IPO.
Hook card: **$81 BILLION IN ONE DAY**
- A: An AI company just raised 81 billion dollars in a single stock sale, the biggest IPO deal ever priced!
- Q: Which one?
- A: Anthropic, the maker of Claude. It listed on Nasdaq in November 2026.
- Q: Who's it racing against?
- A: OpenAI, ChatGPT's maker, valued at about 1.25 trillion dollars.
- A: Google DeepMind in London. Gemini has over a billion users.
- A: DeepSeek in Hangzhou, giving its models away for free.
- A: Mistral in Paris, and A, S, M, L is one of its biggest owners!
- Q: And they all need the same thing?
- A: Chips from Nvidia, made in Taiwan, printed by one Dutch machine.
- A: That's where 81 billion dollars goes.  *(loops / closes the whole series back to Part 1)*
Hidden card: ANTHROPIC IPO: $81.3B RAISED AT $83/SHARE (18 NOV 2026)
